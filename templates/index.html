About This Study

This experiment is part of an ongoing academic research project examining whether background music influences cognitive task performance. Specifically, we use an Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) framework to compare performance across two auditory conditions: silence and instrumental study music.

You will complete two short cognitive tests. The tests measure reasoning, pattern recognition, and attention — not your general intelligence or IQ. There are no trick questions. Performance naturally varies from day to day, so please answer as carefully as you can under the conditions provided.

What to Expect

Test 1 — Completed in silence. 20 multiple-choice questions. 20-minute time limit.

Short Break — A 2-minute rest between tests.

Test 2 — Completed with instrumental background music. 20 multiple-choice questions. 20-minute time limit.

After both tests, you will see your individual results. The final statistical analysis across all participants will be performed separately.

Participant Instructions

  1. Find a quiet place where you will not be interrupted.
  2. Complete Test 1 in complete silence (no music, no background noise if possible).
  3. Take the short break between tests.
  4. For Test 2, use the in-page music player at a comfortable, moderate volume. Use headphones if available.
  5. Answer every question to the best of your ability. You may navigate between questions freely.
  6. The test auto-submits when the timer reaches zero.
  7. Do not refresh the page during a test.

Participant Registration & Consent

Please enter your full name and confirm your consent before starting the experiment.

Your name will be recorded alongside your test performance.

What the study involves: You will complete two cognitive tests as described above.

Data collected: Your name, test answers, scores, and completion time per test.

How data will be used: Results will be analysed statistically as part of the APSP research project.

Withdrawal: You may close the browser at any time. Partial data will not be used if you do not complete both tests.

Note: The tests are not validated psychometric instruments. Results reflect task performance under the given conditions only and should not be interpreted as a measure of intelligence or ability.
